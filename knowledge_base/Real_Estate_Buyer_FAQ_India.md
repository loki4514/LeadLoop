# Indian Real Estate — Buyer FAQ Knowledge Base

A practical, RAG-ready reference for home buyers, sellers, and renters in India.
Each entry is self-contained so it can be retrieved and answered independently.
Figures (interest rates, LTV, stamp duty, tax limits) reflect commonly applicable
norms and can vary by lender, state, and year — always confirm current values with
the specific bank or state authority.

---

## What are the eligibility criteria for a home loan in India?

Home loan eligibility is assessed on a few core factors:

- **Age:** Typically 21 to 65 years. The loan must usually be fully repaid before
  the borrower turns 70 (salaried) or 65–70 (self-employed).
- **Income:** A stable, verifiable income. Most lenders require a minimum net
  monthly income of about ₹25,000–₹40,000, though this varies by city and lender.
- **Employment:** Salaried applicants generally need 2+ years of work history;
  self-employed applicants need 3+ years of business continuity with filed ITRs.
- **Credit score (CIBIL):** 750 or above is considered strong; 650–750 may be
  approved at higher interest; below 650 is often rejected or heavily priced.
- **FOIR / debt-to-income:** Existing EMIs plus the proposed EMI should not exceed
  roughly 40–55% of net monthly income.
- **Property:** The property must have clear, marketable title and approved plans;
  the lender independently values it.

Co-applicants (spouse, parents) can be added to boost eligibility.

## How is home loan EMI calculated?

EMI (Equated Monthly Instalment) is calculated with the standard amortization
formula:

`EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n / ((1 + r)^n − 1)`

where **P** is the principal, **r** is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100),
and **n** is the tenure in months.

Example: on a ₹50,00,000 loan at 9% annual interest for 20 years (240 months),
the EMI is approximately **₹44,986**. A longer tenure lowers the EMI but increases
total interest paid; a shorter tenure does the opposite.

## What is LTV (Loan-to-Value) and how much can I borrow against a property?

LTV is the share of the property's value a lender will finance. RBI guidelines cap it by loan size:

- Loans up to **₹30 lakh:** up to **90%** LTV.
- Loans **₹30–75 lakh:** up to **80%** LTV.
- Loans above **₹75 lakh:** up to **75%** LTV.

The remaining amount is the buyer's **down payment**. Note that LTV applies to the
property value or agreement value (whichever is lower), and does not include stamp
duty and registration charges, which the buyer must fund separately.

## What is the difference between a fixed and a floating interest rate home loan?

- **Fixed rate:** The interest rate stays constant for a defined period (or the full
  tenure). EMIs are predictable, but fixed rates are usually 1–2% higher and you
  don't benefit when market rates fall.
- **Floating rate:** The rate moves with a benchmark (currently the RBI repo rate
  under the external benchmark lending rate regime). EMIs or tenure adjust as rates
  change. Floating rates are generally lower and have no/low prepayment penalty for
  individuals.

Most home buyers in India choose floating-rate loans.

## What documents are required to apply for a home loan?

Typical documentation:

- **Identity & address:** PAN card (mandatory), Aadhaar, passport, voter ID, or
  driving licence.
- **Income (salaried):** Last 3 months' salary slips, last 6 months' bank
  statements, Form 16 / last 2 years' ITR.
- **Income (self-employed):** Last 2–3 years' ITR with computation, audited
  financials, business proof, GST returns, bank statements.
- **Property documents:** Sale agreement, title deed, approved building plan,
  allotment letter, NOC from builder/society, encumbrance certificate.
- **Others:** Passport-size photos, processing fee cheque, and existing loan
  statements if any.

## What is a foreclosure or prepayment of a home loan, and are there charges?

Foreclosure means repaying the entire outstanding loan before the end of the
tenure; prepayment means paying a lump sum to reduce the principal. As per RBI
rules, **floating-rate home loans to individuals carry no prepayment or foreclosure
charges.** Fixed-rate loans may attract a charge of around 2–4% of the outstanding
amount. Prepaying early in the tenure saves the most interest, since early EMIs are
interest-heavy.

## What is a home loan balance transfer?

A balance transfer moves your outstanding home loan from one lender to another —
usually to get a lower interest rate. Benefits include reduced EMI or tenure and
sometimes a top-up loan. Costs to weigh: processing fees at the new lender (0.25–1%),
legal/valuation charges, and paperwork. A balance transfer is generally worthwhile
when the rate difference is at least 0.5–1% and significant tenure remains.

## Who can be a co-applicant on a home loan?

A co-applicant shares repayment responsibility and can increase loan eligibility.
Common co-applicants are spouse, parents, or (in some cases) siblings and children.
All co-owners of the property must be co-applicants, but a co-applicant need not be
a co-owner. Adding an earning co-applicant pools incomes for a higher eligible loan,
and both applicants can claim separate tax deductions if they are co-owners and
co-borrowers.

## What property documents should a buyer verify before purchase?

Key documents to check for clear title and legality:

- **Title deed / sale deed:** Establishes ownership and the chain of title.
- **Mother deed:** Traces the property's ownership history.
- **Encumbrance certificate (EC):** Confirms the property is free of legal dues or
  mortgages (obtain for the last 13–30 years).
- **Khata / Patta:** Municipal/revenue record of the property in the owner's name.
- **Occupancy certificate (OC) & Completion certificate (CC):** Confirm the building
  is legally built and fit for occupation.
- **Approved building plan / layout:** Sanctioned by the local authority.
- **Property tax receipts:** Up to date, no arrears.
- **RERA registration:** For under-construction projects.
- **NOCs:** From bank, society, and relevant authorities.

## What is RERA and how does it protect home buyers?

RERA (Real Estate Regulation and Development Act, 2016) regulates the real estate
sector to protect buyers. Key protections:

- Developers must **register projects** with the state RERA authority before
  advertising or selling.
- Builders must deposit **70% of buyer funds** in a dedicated escrow account, used
  only for that project's construction.
- Sale is based on **carpet area**, not super built-up area.
- Developers are liable for **structural defects for 5 years** after handover.
- Penalties and interest apply for **delayed possession**.
- Buyers can file complaints with the RERA authority for quick redressal.

## What is the process of registering a property in India?

Property registration is mandatory under the Registration Act, 1908:

1. **Verify title** and prepare the sale deed on the required stamp paper.
2. **Pay stamp duty and registration fees** (online or via challan).
3. **Book an appointment** at the Sub-Registrar's office where the property is located.
4. Buyer, seller, and two witnesses appear with original documents and ID proofs.
5. **Biometrics/photographs** are captured; the deed is executed and registered.
6. Collect the **registered sale deed** (usually within a few days to weeks).
7. **Mutation:** Update the property records (khata) to the new owner's name.

## What is stamp duty and how much is it?

Stamp duty is a state tax paid on property transactions to make the sale deed
legally valid. Rates vary by state, typically **4% to 8%** of the property's
agreement value or circle rate (whichever is higher). Many states offer a **1–2%
concession for women buyers.** In addition, a **registration fee of about 1%**
(often capped) applies. For example, in many states a woman buyer pays around 4–5%
stamp duty versus 5–7% for men. Always check your specific state's current rate.

## What is circle rate, guidance value, and market value?

- **Circle rate / guidance value / ready reckoner rate:** The minimum government-set
  value per unit area below which a property cannot be registered. Terminology varies
  by state (circle rate in Delhi, guidance value in Karnataka, ready reckoner in
  Maharashtra).
- **Market value:** The actual price the property commands based on demand, location,
  and amenities.

Stamp duty is charged on the higher of the two. If market value is below circle rate,
buyer and seller may still owe tax on the circle-rate value.

## What taxes and deductions apply to home loans and property?

- **Section 80C:** Deduction up to ₹1.5 lakh/year on home loan **principal** repayment
  (plus stamp duty and registration in the year paid).
- **Section 24(b):** Deduction up to ₹2 lakh/year on home loan **interest** for a
  self-occupied property.
- **Section 80EEA:** Additional interest deduction up to ₹1.5 lakh for eligible
  affordable-housing first-time buyers (subject to conditions and timelines).
- **Capital gains:** Profit on sale is taxed as short-term (held ≤ 24 months, at slab
  rate) or long-term (held > 24 months, at 20% with indexation). Exemptions available
  under Sections 54 / 54EC by reinvesting in another house or specified bonds.
- **TDS:** The buyer must deduct **1% TDS** on property purchases of ₹50 lakh or more
  and deposit it against the seller's PAN.
- **GST:** Applies to under-construction property (currently 1% for affordable, 5%
  for other, without input tax credit). No GST on ready-to-move or resale property.

## What are the steps involved in buying a property?

1. **Budgeting & financial planning** — determine affordability and down payment.
2. **Loan pre-approval** — check eligibility and get an in-principle sanction.
3. **Property search & site visits** — shortlist by location, configuration, price.
4. **Negotiation** — agree on price and terms with the seller/builder.
5. **Legal due diligence** — verify title, EC, approvals, RERA registration.
6. **Technical due diligence** — check construction quality, plan approval.
7. **Agreement to sell** — sign and pay the booking/token amount.
8. **Loan disbursal** — lender releases funds after document verification.
9. **Registration** — pay stamp duty, register the sale deed.
10. **Possession & mutation** — take handover and update municipal records.

## What is the difference between carpet area, built-up area, and super built-up area?

- **Carpet area:** The actual usable floor area within the walls of the unit. RERA
  mandates pricing on carpet area.
- **Built-up area:** Carpet area plus the area of internal walls and balconies
  (roughly 10–15% more than carpet area).
- **Super built-up area:** Built-up area plus a share of common areas — lobby,
  stairs, lift, amenities (the "loading factor," often 25–40% over carpet area).

Always confirm the carpet area, as super built-up figures can inflate the apparent
size.

## What is the difference between freehold and leasehold property?

- **Freehold:** The buyer owns the property and the land outright, indefinitely, with
  full rights to sell, transfer, or modify (subject to local rules). Preferred by
  most buyers.
- **Leasehold:** The buyer holds the property for a fixed lease term (e.g. 30, 99, or
  999 years) from a lessor (often a government body). The land reverts to the lessor
  at lease end unless renewed; transfers may need lessor consent.

Freehold generally commands a higher value and is easier to finance and resell.

## What should I know before renting or leasing a property?

- **Rental agreement:** Get a written agreement; register it if the term exceeds 11
  months (most are 11-month leases to avoid mandatory registration).
- **Security deposit:** Typically 1–3 months' rent (up to 10 months in some cities);
  refundable, minus damages.
- **Maintenance:** Clarify who pays society maintenance and for what repairs.
- **Notice period:** Usually 1–2 months for either party to terminate.
- **Rent escalation:** Commonly 5–10% annually, stated in the agreement.
- **Responsibilities:** Landlord handles major/structural repairs; tenant handles
  minor upkeep and utility bills unless agreed otherwise.

## What is an encumbrance certificate and why does it matter?

An encumbrance certificate (EC) is a legal document issued by the Sub-Registrar's
office that shows all registered transactions on a property over a requested period
— sales, mortgages, and liens. A **clear (nil) EC** confirms the property has no
outstanding loans or legal dues and has clean title, which is essential for buyers
and lenders. Buyers typically obtain an EC covering the last 13 to 30 years before
purchase.

## What due diligence should a buyer do before finalizing a purchase?

- Verify the **chain of title** and that the seller is the rightful owner.
- Obtain and review the **encumbrance certificate** for the required period.
- Confirm **approved plans, OC/CC, and RERA registration**.
- Check that **property tax and utility bills** are cleared.
- Verify the seller's **identity and, if applicable, power of attorney**.
- Confirm there is **no ongoing litigation** on the property.
- For resale, get a **NOC from the society and bank** (if under mortgage).
- Engage a **lawyer** for title verification and a **valuer/engineer** for technical checks.
